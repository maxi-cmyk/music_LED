How the FFT reaches the same X faster

  1. Split the samples into even and odd

    is a DFT of the 64 even samples, a DFT of the 64 odd samples. Each is half the size of the original problem.

  2. Twiddle factor
  3. Butterfly: one rotation, two outputs
  4. Split again until one sample is left
    1. 1 DFT of 128
    2. 2 of 64
    3. 4 of 32
    4. 8 of 16
    5. 16 of 8
    6. 32 of 4
    7. 64 of 2
    8. 128 of 1

    A DFT of one sample is the sample itself. The FFT starts there and runs the butterflies back up: 7 levels () of 64 butterflies each, 448 in total, instead of weighted samples for the direct DFT.
